# The Bonded Beasts: The Four Classes of War

> *"A dinosaur without a rider is dangerous. A rider without a dinosaur is weak. But together? Together they're a tactical advantage—if you can survive the bonding process."*  
> — From the **Rider's Manual, Fourth Edition**

---

## I. The Reality of the Bond

In the world after the Fracture, dinosaurs are not pets. They're **weapons systems** with their own survival instincts, territorial behaviors, and—thanks to Essence exposure—unsettling intelligence.

Bonding isn't about love. It's about **mutual benefit**:
- The human provides strategy, tool use, and access to resources
- The dinosaur provides mobility, strength, and combat capability

It works. Barely. And every rider knows: your mount isn't loyal out of affection.

It's loyal because you're useful.

---

## II. The Ronin Raptor: Expendable Speed

### The Velociraptor

**Faction Preference**: Smugglers, though every faction uses them

**Role**: Scout, infiltrator, first casualty

### The Philosophy: Fast and Forgotten

Raptors are cheap. Abundant. Easy to replace.

Which means Raptor riders have the shortest life expectancy of any class. You're the one sent ahead to trip the traps, test the defenses, draw enemy fire.

**Raptor Rider Wisdom**: *"Die fast or get promoted. Those are your options."*

### The Bond: Convenience, Not Connection

Bonding with a Raptor is simple: find a juvenile, feed it regularly, don't get bitten, congratulations you have a mount.

There's no spiritual ritual. No test of worthiness. Raptors bond with anyone who provides food and doesn't  immediately get them killed.

**Why it works**: Raptors in the wild have short lifespans. A human partnership doubles their survival odds. They're not stupid.

### Training: Stay Alive Long Enough to Learn

Raptor training is brutal and efficient:
- **Week 1**: Learn to ride without falling off
- **Week 2**: Learn to scout without being seen
- **Week 3**: Half your class is dead. Congratulations, you're experienced.

The Wall Leap ability (climbing vertical surfaces) isn't taught—it's **discovered**. Raptors naturally climb when fleeing predators. Smart riders figure out how to use it tactically.

Dumb riders die before they get the chance.

### Famous Example: Captain Yuki "The Lucky"

Yuki survived seven Raptor deaths. Each time, she bonded with a new one within 24 hours. When asked how she kept going, she said: *"Raptors are expendable. I'm not."*

She died on her eighth mount. Enemy sniper. The Raptor survived and bonded with someone else the next day.

## III. The Shogun T-Rex: Expensive Power

### The Tyrannosaurus Rex

**Faction Preference**: Warlords, Fortress (when they can afford it)

**Role**: Frontline devastation, status symbol, logistical nightmare

### The Philosophy: Power Has a Price

T-Rexes are **rare**. Expensive to hatch. Expensive to feed. Expensive to armor.

One T-Rex eats more in a week than ten Raptors eat in a month. They require specialized handlers, custom equipment, and constant Essence supplementation to maintain bonding.

**T-Rex Rider Reality**: *"My mount costs more than your entire squad. I will not risk it for your bad tactics."*

### The Bond: Earned Through Resources

A T-Rex won't bond with just anyone. It bonds with whoever controls **food supply**.

In practice, this means only high-ranking officers or wealthy traders can afford to keep one. The "trial by sacrifice" is real—but it's economic sacrifice, not moral sacrifice.

You want a T-Rex? Prove you can feed it. That's the test.

### Training: Learning to Justify the Expense

T-Rex riders train in:
- **Formation tactics**: How to maximize battlefield impact
- **Resource management**: How to prove your mount's ROI to commanders
- **Political maneuvering**: How to keep your mount when budgets get cut

The War Cry ability is useful, but the real value of a T-Rex is **intimidation**. Enemy forces see one and often retreat without fighting.

Which saves resources. Which justifies the cost. Mostly.

### Famous Example: General Takeshi "Ironbudget"

Takeshi's T-Rex held a pass for six hours, yes. But what the stories don't mention: he disobeyed retreat orders to do it, and his mount took critical damage that cost the Fortress three months of Essence reserves to heal.

He was demoted. The civilians he saved didn't compensate him for his financial losses.

But hey, there's a statue. That's something.

## IV. The Ninja Pteranodon: Fragile Intelligence

### The Pteranodon

**Faction Preference**: Shrine (they breed them), anyone who can steal eggs

**Role**: Reconnaissance, communication, target practice

### The Philosophy: See Everything, Survive Nothing

Pteranodons are smart. Too smart. They understand battlefield dynamics better than most human commanders.

They're also **fragile as hell**. One good shot and they're down. No armor can cover wings without ruining flight capability.

**Pteranodon Rider Wisdom**: *"You will see your death coming from a mile away. Won't help."*

### The Bond: Intellectual Partnership

Pteranodons bond based on cognitive compatibility. They assess potential riders through problem-solving tests—spatial reasoning, pattern recognition, tactical thinking.

If you're too dumb, the Pteranodon won't bond. It's not cruel. It's practical. A stupid rider gets them killed.

The "leap of faith" bonding ritual exists, but it's not about trust—it's about physics. The Pteranodon calculates if it can catch you based on weight, wind conditions, and trajectory. If the math doesn't work, it lets you fall.

Natural selection for riders who check their weight before jumping.

### Training: Mathematics and Mourning

Pteranodon riders have the highest education requirements:
- Meteorology (wind patterns affect flight)
- Cartography (aerial navigation)
- Ballistics (dodging is easier if you know bullet trajectories)

They also have the highest casualty rates after Raptors. Recon missions in contested airspace are suicide runs.

Most Pteranodon riders cycle through 3-4 mounts before they die or quit. The Pteranodons cycle through more riders than that.

### Famous Example: Hana "Skywhisper"

Hana's seven-day siege reconnaissance was effective, yes. Less mentioned: two other Pteranodon riders died attempting the same mission.

Hana had better math skills. That's the only reason she lived.

She retired after that battle. Couldn't stomach the odds anymore.

## V. The Ashigaru Triceratops: Reliable and Trapped

### The Triceratops

**Faction Preference**: Everyone. They're workhorses.

**Role**: Labor, defense, transportation, everything unglamorous

### The Philosophy: Function Over Glory

Triceratops are the most common bonded beast. Not because they're beloved. Because they're **useful** in ways that don't involve dying gloriously.

They haul cargo. They plow fields. They stand in shield walls. They do the tedious, essential work that keeps factions alive.

**Triceratops Rider Reality**: *"Your Raptor looks cool. My Triceratops built the wall you hide behind."*

### The Bond: Economic Necessity

Triceratops bond easily. Almost too easily. They're herd animals—genetically programmed to follow leadership, accept hierarchy, work cooperatively.

Which makes them vulnerable to exploitation. A Triceratops will work itself to death if commanded. It won't rebel. It won't run.

The "bond through shared labor" sounds noble. In practice, it means: work together  until one of you collapses.

Usually the dinosaur.

### Training: Endurance or Abuse?

Triceratops training is controversially **harsh**:
- Loading them with excessive weight to "build endurance"
- Forcing them to hold position until exhaustion
- Punishing any sign of  independent thought

Defenders call it discipline. Critics call it cruelty.

The Siege Stance ability (hunker down to become a wall) is effective. It's also what happens when a Triceratops is too exhausted to move and has learned that standing in place earns less punishment than trying to flee.

### Famous Example: Kenji "The Unmoved"

Kenji held that chokepoint for six hours. What the stories omit: his Triceratops had stress fractures in both front legs for months afterward.

It never fully healed. Kenji rode it until it could no longer stand, then bonded with a younger one.

The first Triceratops was processed for meat and Essence extraction.

Kenji still has its skull mounted in his quarters. He calls it "remembering sacrifice."

Others call it a trophy.

## VI. The Reality of the Four Classes

The classes aren't philosophies. They're **tactical assets** with different cost-benefit ratios:

- **Raptor**: Cheap, fast, expendable. High casualty rate.
- **T-Rex**: Expensive, powerful, rare. High prestige.
- **Pteranodon**: Intelligent, fragile, specialized. High skill requirement.
- **Triceratops**: Reliable, unglamorous, exploited. High workload.

Factions use all four not because of romantic ideals about "strength through diversity."

They use all four because **specialization wins wars**, and wars demand sacrifices.

The dinosaurs make those sacrifices. They don't have a choice.

The rider do. They make them anyway.
